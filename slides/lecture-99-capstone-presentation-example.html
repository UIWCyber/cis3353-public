Motivation

  • Why it matters: a small accounting business runs a public client portal on the same flat network as the workstations that hold tax records. One compromised web server would expose everything.
  • Who benefits: the owner who must protect client data, and the staff who need the portal to stay online.
  • Current landscape: many small offices run a single flat LAN with a consumer router and no monitoring, so an attacker who lands anywhere can reach everything.
  • The gap: the business has no network segmentation, no intrusion detection, and no hardened wireless. That gap is what SecureDesk closes.

Objective

  • Segment the network into WAN, DMZ, and LAN zones on pfSense so a compromised portal cannot reach the internal workstations.
  • Detect intrusions with a Snort IDS watching perimeter traffic, and harden wireless with WPA3-Enterprise.
  • Prove the design by demonstrating a real attack against the flat network, then showing the same attack blocked and alerted after the defenses are in place.
  • Modules integrated (three required, four used): Module 2 Pervasive Attack Surfaces and Controls, Module 8 Infrastructure Threats and Security Monitoring, Module 9 Infrastructure Security, and Module 10 Wireless Network Attacks and Defenses.

Attack Demonstration, Before Defenses

  • On the flat network (before segmentation), we showed the threat working end to end.
  • Port scan: from Kali, nmap found the web portal and an open management port. Command: nmap -sV 10.10.10.20.
  • Pivot attempt: from the compromised portal host, we reached a LAN workstation because nothing separated the zones. Command: nc -zv 10.10.20.10 445.
  • The pivot succeeded, proving a single web compromise exposed the internal records. This is the risk SecureDesk removes.
NoteShowing the attack succeed against the undefended build is required. If the graders never saw it break, they cannot see that you fixed it.

Challenges and Limitations

  • Challenges (what was hard to build):
  • Getting pfSense to route three interfaces correctly took several tries before DMZ and LAN were truly isolated.
  • Snort produced noisy alerts until we tuned the rule set down to the scans we cared about.
  • WPA3-Enterprise needed a working RADIUS setup, which was the longest single task in the project.
  • Limitations (what we did not solve):
  • The whole build runs on one host in nested virtualization; performance is fine for a demo, not for real traffic.
  • The IDS detects the scans we tuned for; a slow or fragmented scan could still slip past.
  • We hardened the perimeter, but we did not harden the individual endpoints beyond the network layer.

Future Work

1. Add an IPS mode: move Snort from detection to inline prevention so the scan is dropped, not just alerted.

2. Central logging: forward pfSense and Snort logs to a small SIEM so alerts and drops are correlated in one place.

3. Endpoint hardening: extend the project into Module 5 with host firewalls and patch baselines on the DMZ and LAN machines.

Conclusion

  • Outcome: a segmented small-business network where a web-portal compromise can no longer reach internal records, with an IDS that sees the attack and WPA3-Enterprise on the air.
  • What we learned: segmentation and monitoring are the highest-value, lowest-cost defenses for a small office; most of the risk came from the flat network, not from any single exploit.
  • Course modules demonstrated: attack surfaces and controls (Module 2), infrastructure monitoring (Module 8), infrastructure security (Module 9), and wireless defenses (Module 10), all in one build we attacked and then defended.
