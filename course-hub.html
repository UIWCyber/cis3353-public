Capstone: the 35%, broken down

A semester-long team project. Pick a scenario, integrate at least three course modules, and Build (replicate the environment in VMs and virtual networks), Attack (demonstrate the threat), and Defend (implement and verify controls).

Capstone Proposal (approved by instructor)1%
Project Implementation (Build / Attack / Defend, tracked on GitHub)15%
Final Presentation (Week 15)10%
GitHub Wiki Page - the final report9%
Missing your scheduled presentation slot costs an automatic 15% off your team's total capstone grade.
Your repository is the record of your effort. Individual grades scale with verified contribution: only real artifacts count (configs, scripts, screenshots, wiki pages, closed board issues with matching commits). A one-line commit claiming "done on my own device" earns nothing, and end-of-semester bulk commits are investigated. Keep the Individual Contribution Summary Table in your README current. Individual grades are capped per component.

Team GitHub requirements

  • Teams of 2 to 3 students - no solo capstones. Before you commit to a team, compare schedules with your prospective teammates: you will collaborate and learn together all semester, so make sure you share workable meeting times.
  • Create a private team GitHub repository - all advancements and contributions live there.
  • Add gdparra-edu and cyberknowledge as collaborators.
  • Manage all project activity on the repo's integrated GitHub Project board.
  • Write the final report as a GitHub Wiki page; keep presentation materials in the repo.
  • Vote (unanimously) on your commit approach and declare it on the proposal: Individual Commits (everyone pushes their own work; points follow the committer) or Delegated Commits (designated members push for the team; points follow the board assignee). Not unanimous? You default to Individual Commits.
  • Group Project Days: several class sessions (roughly monthly, marked on the schedule) are fully dedicated to capstone coordination with the instructor in the room. Additional ones may be announced on Canvas.
  • Submit the repo + Project board links through the Capstone assignment on Canvas.

Labs, Quizzes & Reading

Everything Runs on Cengage MindTap

All hands-on labs, quizzes, and reading for this course live on the Cengage MindTap platform, reached through Canvas. There are no lab repositories to accept - just your MindTap course.

How to get into MindTap

  1. Open our course in Canvas and click the MindTap link in the course navigation.
  2. Sign in or create your Cengage account and register your access code when prompted (the first-day-of-class free trial covers you until you purchase).
  3. Your labs, quizzes, and readings appear on the MindTap Learning Path, organized by module.
  4. Work through each item there. Due dates are posted on Canvas - check the Canvas calendar and announcements, not just MindTap.

What lives where

  • Hands-on Labs, Quizzes, and Reading: MindTap via Canvas.
  • Due dates and announcements: Canvas.
  • The semester-long capstone: your team's GitHub repository (see the Capstone section).
  • The public course site (this hub, the outline, capstone decks and forms): uiwcyber.github.io/cis3353-public.
Your machine: for the capstone you will build environments in virtual machines. Install VirtualBox or VMware, and pull the images your chosen project needs - commonly Kali Linux and pfSense. MindTap labs run in the browser and need nothing beyond a current browser.

The Fine Print, Digested

Policies You'll Actually Ask About

The full versions live in the course outline and syllabus on Canvas - this is the quick reference.

Late work

  • Labs, quizzes, and the capstone proposal lose 15% per calendar day late (grade floor of 0).
  • Capstone final deliverables are not accepted late: the Final Report wiki, the GitHub repository, the GitHub Project board, and the Final Presentation earn a zero for that component if not delivered on time.
  • Documented emergency? Contact Dr. Parra before the deadline - accommodations are case by case at the instructor's discretion.

Attendance

  • Attendance is mandatory and worth 10%. Up to 3 absences without penalty, excused or not. The 4th absence forfeits the entire 10%.
  • Arriving 10+ minutes late counts as tardy; 3 tardies = 1 unexcused absence.
  • Planned university/religious/military absences: written notice at least two weeks ahead, and they still count toward the three. Four or more absences without officially dropping results in a failing grade per university policy.

Academic integrity

Highest standards of academic honesty apply. Cheating and plagiarism are handled per the UIW Student Handbook (Section 5, Part D). In a course about security, integrity is the discipline - your capstone commit history leaves an audit trail, and so do you.

This outline is a guideline

The schedule and procedures may change under extenuating circumstances or when it benefits the class. Prominent changes are announced through Canvas Announcements - check it and your university email regularly; that's the official channel.
